Deferred by the tanto-sweep spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-10-tanto-sweep-design.md`,
"Deferred items" 4). The dialogue's D-6 chose **invocation over generation**
for a task's Verify step: the step becomes one call of
`node "$TANTO/scripts/passage-check.js" verify --plan <path> --task <N>`,
and the needles, anchor values, and line counts that call checks are all
determined by the blocks the plan already carries (issue-f813). A generator
that would emit the blocks themselves — from a diff, from a spec's labelled
blocks, or from an edit made by hand and recorded — was not discussed in the
dialogue and is not proposed by the spec.

Why it is recorded rather than dropped: the sweep's own authoring measured
that hand-written leads are wrong about one time in four (five count defects
in twenty-two leads, all the same off-by-one from a blank line inside a
block), and that a thirty-line checker found every one before the commit.
The instrument now closes the checking half. Whether the authoring half —
writing the lead line, counting the block, placing the anchor — should also
be a tool, and what its input would be, is the open question. Two inputs
suggest themselves and each has a cost: a generator fed by the spec's
labelled blocks makes the spec the only hand-written source, which moves the
count defects one document up rather than removing them; a generator fed by
an edit already made to a scratch copy (`git diff` of the copy against the
base) produces exact blocks but inverts the plan's order of authorship, since
the passage would exist before the plan that carries it.

Nothing fails today: `lint` catches what a generator would prevent, at the
cost of one turn per defect. Decide when a second passage plan has run under
the instrument and the count of `lint` findings per plan is a number rather
than a guess.

Related: issue-7481 (the instrument), issue-f813 (each block once, cited by
id), issue-88d3 (the count and anchor halves the instrument closes),
design-4807 (the passage-plan conventions), req-04f5.
